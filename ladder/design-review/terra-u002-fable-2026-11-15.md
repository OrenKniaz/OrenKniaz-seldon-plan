## 2. The one rule
**Rule 1. Unit stage is closed over the worktree. Everything demanded of a unit (brief, judge target, failure text) must be satisfiable inside its scope, and only code proof says whether it is finished. A model's statement that something lies outside ("blocked", "declare", "provide the integrated checkout", contract verdict `user`) is a routing fact for code (WAVE-VERIFY note, CONTRACT, owner fixup). It is never a failure, a recovery or an owner question.**
This is speed-design §10.2 completed, and it reverses my own §10.4 ("self-owned blocked asks at once: keep"). That rule assumed a worker's "blocked" is true. With a weak
worker it is the default way to say "less than perfect". Code can test the claim for free: PROVING runs the unit checks at P. Passing = it was done. Failing = an exact,
code-observed failure for the same worker. A genuine self-block now costs two reworks before the question; in exchange no model answer can open a question alone.
Holds at 100 units: no new state, no counter, fewer branches.

## 3. Second rule (separate: convergence of the unit JUDGE)
**Rule 2. Source is judged once (R2). A re-judgment after rework is closed: it asks only whether the first judgment's named gaps are closed; code keeps the earlier passes.**

## 4. Is a UNIT owner question for a judge gap legitimate? No.
The sim owner did not wander; the judge did. Every sim answer is a paraphrase of that round's gap or self-block: r606~r601, r641~r620, r695~r690, r740~r720, u03 r373~r366,
u04 r248~r243, r356~r343, u05 r262~r257. The owner adds zero information, and the answer is then appended to the failure text while the next judge raises something else.
"Is this check meaningful" is the judge's subject (R9); an owner cannot know it. Owner questions that remain legitimate: a commitment change, accept/drop/redirect at the
end of a chain of **code-observed** failures, and acceptance at CHECK-IN/C on the real product.
