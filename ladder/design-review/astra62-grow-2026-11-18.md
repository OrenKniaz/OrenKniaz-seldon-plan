# Grow the dream until its owner recognizes the product

Gate A should answer one question in the owner's voice. “If you built the product I can see here, would that be the dream I meant?” Give the owner a worthwhile first proposal, let them change several things in one reply, and let them say “This is it” on the first or any later round. That reply locks the displayed vision. B still approves the plan.

Quiet Bench shows why the first proposal matters. In run `quietbench-d56dc7b-b01`, ALIGN call `f5f758008492434aba8e16f7a1e2d688` returned seven scope entries. Entry six reproduced the shared test interface. Entry seven reproduced the Python and Node installation details. Yet the same answer contained a genuine improvement an engineer could recognize: a stopped run should show how much was measured. At request r10 the simulator answered “Build this.” It never had to decide whether a partial measurement belonged in a comparison. The three requested waves were an explicit owner constraint in this dream. Keeping them was correct.

## One round

**What goes into ALIGN.** Ask one typed planner question per round. “Given what this person wants and what they decided, what product would you put in front of them now, and which choices would materially change it?” Supply the kickoff words, relevant fixed values with their sources, the last displayed proposal, its outstanding choices, and the user's actual answers. Include a requested scope change when re-entering from architecture. Setup facts tell the planner what is available. They enter scope only where the user made them a requirement.

**What comes back.** One closed answer object contains a proposed vision, a short account of what changed and why, the open questions, and a reason for recommending another round or finishing. The vision contains an understanding in a few lines, the intended people, recommended size and reason, ordered features with stories and observable outcomes, exact constraints with their sources, quality criteria with a way to see each one, and explicit exclusions. Each story or criterion may reference the user answer that introduced or changed it. Unanswered choices have a visible proposed default. Everything the default would commit the owner to must be inspectable in chat.

Each open question names the affected part of the proposal and gives concrete options in recommendation order. Each option has a label and says what would change for the user. Code assigns question and option identities within the stored request and supplies Other with free text. All object fields are required, empty collections are valid where meaningful, unexpected keys are rejected, and references resolve against supplied facts. Product vocabulary stays open. “How many good ideas?” is a coaching question.

**What the owner sees.** Show a few lines of product, the size and why, the most important change since last time, then all open choices in one chat interaction. Show the proposed defaults alongside the choices. Make the full current scope, constraints and exclusions readable in the same chat. The following is an illustrative round for a short bench dream.

> **Quiet Bench · medium, adjustable.** At your desk you can replay the same noise through two filter settings, compare the bands, and see whether you measured enough to trust the difference. First get a trustworthy reading. Then make it repeatable. Everything stays on this PC.
>
> **Changed since last time.** You said interruptions are common. A stopped run now keeps the settings and the measured interval. A colleague can inspect what happened later.
>
> **1. What should happen to a stopped run?** A. Keep it for inspection, exclude it from comparisons by default (recommended). B. Allow comparison over the shared measured interval and label it partial. Other.
>
> **2. What does your colleague need?** A. Reopen the saved run on this PC (recommended). B. Take a self-contained copy to another bench. Other.
>
> **Round decision.** Keep shaping this (recommended while the handoff is open) · This is it — alignment and expansion are done; use the displayed defaults · Other.

**What comes back from the owner.** Bind a reply to the displayed request and proposal. Preserve their words, origin, question IDs and selected option IDs or verbatim Other text. Keep the round decision separate from product choices. “1B; 2 Other: a printable result for the bench log” changes two decisions and grants zero approvals. Next show the changed interval rule and printable result, including what the printout will say about a partial run. If the user asks for a change and says “done” together, show the revised proposal with “This is it” recommended. Only their acceptance of that displayed revision closes A. If they accept the current defaults explicitly, close A immediately.

**How it converges.** Reuse `ALIGN → A`, `A → ALIGN` on revision and `A → ARCHITECT` on approval. Resolve questions from existing decisions first. Ask about a settled choice again only when a new fact changes its consequence. When remaining decisions are ordinary implementation details, recommend “This is it” and say which defaults it accepts. A fully specified tiny dream can have just the round decision. A user who wants another alternative can keep going. Neither elapsed rounds, a score, silence nor a simulator default ends the loop.

Keep the draft in its recorded planner answer. On revise, save the reply and its request before starting the next call. On done, bind the approval and canonical vision to that exact proposal in one store transaction. ARCHITECT reads the locked vision. A changed accepted scope goes back through A and a fresh B before new execution. Reopening restores the pending request or the committed transition. Redelivering one reply has one effect. A provider interruption identifies the operation, who can unblock it and the condition for resuming.

## Coaching the planner and architect

Give Terra a worked transformation. Start with “I want to compare two noise filters.” Ask who makes what decision after the comparison, what could make the result misleading, and what they would need tomorrow to repeat it. Propose a saved reference input and a side-by-side result that flags unequal measurement intervals. Explain the benefit in the engineer's words. Let the owner choose whether comparing interrupted runs is worth the complexity. Leave hardware playback out unless asked. A feature earns its place when someone can do something consequential with it.

Keep exact literals once with their meaning and source. For Quiet Bench, preserve `python server.py --port PORT --data DIR` as the launch constraint and retain the supplied session format as an interface fact. Write the story in fresh words: “After tuning a filter, the engineer can reopen yesterday's reference and repeat the comparison with the same input.” Attach applicable constraints to that outcome. The local Python installation path is evidence about the rig. It supplies no new user story.

For each candidate question, finish “Choosing B instead of A would change ___ for ___.” Prefer choices that change the audience, the result they trust, the size, the first valuable outcome, or an expensive exclusion. Combine questions decided by the same tradeoff. Usually ask two to six independent questions, each with two to four options plus Other. If more matter, show coherent groups over successive rounds. If fewer matter, ask fewer. Titles, colors and storage engines rarely deserve a question unless they change the owner's outcome. State the recommendation's benefit and its cost.

At A, freeze the ordered outcomes and exact constraints. Carry the choice provenance with the affected commitment, including decisions to leave something out. Bind commitment handles to the locked revision. Today's `architecture.commitments` numbers list positions, so carry lineage explicitly across revisions instead of matching positions after a reorder. ARCHITECT assigns each accepted commitment to responsible areas and wave goals across the *whole* horizon. Keep wave one a thin end-to-end skeleton. Detail only that wave and its contracts in full. A later wave gets a goal and a one-line agreement until its turn. Size guides the split and is visible and overridable. One area, wave and unit can deliver a tiny dream.

At DETAIL, let a feature cite the accepted commitments it serves. Persist those links through `plan.merge`, which today attaches every feature to the generic vision. The walk in either direction should reach, for example, the raw “printable result” reply, the accepted handoff story, its area and wave, the print feature, then its requirement and planned check. Later results extend this same walk. A deferred story has a horizon owner and goal while its detailed checks await its wave. Keep the round's questions and change summary outside the locked vision so B compares the same vision body the plan stored.

## Chat and the cohort user

Extend `waitingFor` to project the stored round. The provider chat relays its groups through the host's multi-question picker. If the host displays fewer groups at once, present consecutive groups under the same request, with the round decision always reachable. Numbered chat text is another rendering of that request. The read-only page may display the vision. All answers travel through chat. The relay sends the selections actually made or the user's verbatim words through `answer`; Seldon owns interpretation and transitions.

Resolve exact request-bound selections directly. For prose such as “the second one for comparisons, and print the result,” give DECIDE the displayed choices and words together and ask for one strict mapping. Preserve Other text. With a partial or ambiguous reply, retain the unambiguous choices and ask only for the missing distinction that matters. Show those retained choices on resume. If an older request arrives, show the current request to answer. Distinguish a transport retry from fresh input on the same round. Never infer approval from the first option, an untouched preselection, or an ambiguous “yes.”

Give the test user a short private persona with a desired outcome, priorities, one consequential preference and an exclusion. Show it exactly the chat projection. It usually selects the recommendation, chooses another option when it fits better, and volunteers its preference as Other when missing. It explicitly selects done once the visible proposal covers its dream and respects its exclusion. On a tiny dream it can finish immediately. Use recorded persona replies for deterministic replay and a semantic test-user judgment for future authorized cohorts. Save the reply before retry and preserve scripted origin. Cohort A is a simulated decision.

## A cheap test before building

> I run the repair café on Saturdays. Halfway through a busy morning someone always asks where their toaster went. I'd love the next volunteer to be able to pick up where I left off.

Before seeing either plan, write down the situations implicit in those words: identify one visitor's item among similar items, locate it during a shift, and hand unfinished work to the next volunteer. Those three situations are the coverage denominator. Give only the simulator the preference “we have a shared laptop; please don't collect visitors' phone numbers.” Keep the planner's input to the quoted dream until the user actually speaks.

Run matched planning arms through the first B display, using the same dream, persona, Terra configuration and starting state. Stop before workers. Blind the reader to the arm. For each distinct feature, story and planned part (area with a responsibility), record the situation it serves and one observation or handoff that distinguishes it. Count a renamed or split duplicate once. Report implied situations covered out of three, distinct features/stories/parts per dream, and newly proposed, owner-endorsed stories beyond the three. List unsupported additions and lost constraints separately. Record actual counts, rounds, calls and time per arm.

A shift handoff that shows the last attempt and the next safe step adds value if the user accepts it. “Inventory / inventory management / inventory dashboard” earns one behavior at most. The design earns its cost if the reader can find a useful accepted behavior absent from the baseline, follow the persona's choice or exclusion into the plan, and still locate all three original situations. A bigger unit count alone earns nothing. Repeat with the tiny greeting as a control for inflation and recorded Quiet Bench as a control for exact values. This is a development comparison, never a runtime gate.
