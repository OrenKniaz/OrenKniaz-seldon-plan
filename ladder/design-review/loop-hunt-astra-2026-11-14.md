**One shared rule: unfinished work keeps its recovery history until its owning acceptance check resolves it; moving, renaming, splitting, committing, reopening or answering does not make it new work.**
Normalise every retry, discovery, correction and fixup onto its originating accepted subject and causal record. Preserve that link across owners and children; code owns links/counts and normalises structural differences, while the existing planner/judge owns whether a changed semantic problem is genuinely new. Never use English-string equality or a commit change as the sole escape from escalation; commit/tree identity still owns proof reuse.
Every repeated own-work failure consumes that history: at Oren's second failed work round, route to one user question now; when steering exists, route through it, and its second visit for this work goes to one user question. A local DONE does not erase an unresolved final check/flow. Limits route; they neither fail a run nor shrink its accepted promise.
Keep contract/DETAIL direct questions fixed-point-only: same normalised outline or corrections again asks with the attempted correction. Changing proposals or endless splitting must still spend the shared routing allowance and reach steering; they cannot claim progress merely by changing names. The second steering visit is the §3.2 unresolved-work question, not a refusal to process a contract at an arbitrary count. Without this independent routing bound, equality-only checks cannot guarantee termination for infinitely different proposals.
A retry answer authorises one bounded continuation of the SAME work. On recurrence, show one pending question saying “This is the same unresolved issue after your previous answer,” linking that answer, actual attempts, evidence and recommendation; await fresh words. Never silently replay an answer or replenish a full allowance. A user may deliberately keep requesting attempts; the guarantee is bounded autonomy and truthful waiting, not forced success. Genuine provider unavailability remains a named resumable pause.
