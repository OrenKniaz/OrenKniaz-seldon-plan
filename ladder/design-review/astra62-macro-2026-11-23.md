## Inferred — where the design fights the goal

**The guard can inherit the planner’s blind spot.** DoorCount’s reviewer tightened the retry check. The guard lost a reply, reloaded and confirmed exactly one Out. Neither connected *uncertain tap* with *Mara closes the night*. The guard question supplies B’s subjects and earlier observations. It has no separate field for Mara’s original account. A fresh agent following the same example can reproduce the same omission. [Plan review][rev] · [Guard’s actual actions][g2] · [Current guard question][judge] · [Blind counterexample][b]

**The planner confused staging with sizing.** Mara wanted one door working first. ALIGN estimated days for a plain agent and Lead commissioned three milestone sessions. Plain built the sequence in four minutes. Lead resumed its writer for a repair, then started a fresh session at the next milestone. On main the ordinary next step advances to the next listed outcome. Keep an owner-requested checkpoint. Let what the owner learns there determine whether the same writer continues, the plan changes, or a handoff is worth its cost. [Original sequence][door] · [ALIGN estimate][align] · [Builder sessions][dcalls] · [Milestone advance][leadpy]

**The proof process became a second product.** ShelfLife asked for a short README. Control’s try-it guide opens with Node, a browser kit and an evidence directory before telling a householder how to start the pantry. Four of five morning repair turns changed only guide or check files. One repair concerned recording the exact helper invocation. In w402 the guard spotted a check that claimed an imported row survived restart while asserting only on a different row. Keep executable checks that could detect a real regression. Drop the universal requirement that a customer guide also produce a machine-readable history of proving itself. Main’s v0.5 already lets independent observations carry a weak check forward. ## Inferred — the simplest Lead I would build

1. **Shape the dream with the person.** DoorCount’s ALIGN asked zero questions. One worth asking was whether “the last four Fridays” means four Friday shifts or every shift between two dates. Offer a concrete improvement and let the owner decide. A locks the vision. Independently challenge one spec covering the outcome, constraints, necessary interfaces and what would reveal failure. B approves it before coding. Detail the next usable result and leave later choices open. Scripted assent leaves the value of this conversation unmeasured. [Lead charter C6/C8/C28][charter] · [DoorCount ALIGN][align] · [Plan review][rev]
2. **Give one builder the approved job.** The provider owns execution and concurrency. The builder owns the product, meaningful regression checks and a usable guide. Seldon owns the approved scope, candidate, safe resume and a short handoff with the owner’s words, their reasons, what works and what is open. Remove duplicate restatements of the promise and automatic session changes where a checkpoint suffices. [Lead charter C12/C18/C19][charter] · [Builder inputs and session selection][leadpy] · [DoorCount calls][dcalls]
3. **Make Seldon own the experience.** Give the guard the original dream. Let it choose a realistic journey and interruption, then reconcile what it saw with B and the reusable checks. Tell the owner where the experience felt awkward and what remains unproved. Route a reproduced defect to the builder, a missing observation to the guard and a scope decision to the owner. For maintainability, ask how the next developer would change a rule such as night ownership. Both DoorCount interfaces had lines over 2,200 characters. C is the owner’s decision on this candidate. [Bound proof and carry][blue] · [Search repair][fix] · [Blind maintenance review][walk]

## Inferred — what changes at week scale

The first conversation and approval have a fixed floor. Briefing and check-ins grow roughly with the number of deliveries. If each of M milestones adds one equally costly journey and each replays them all, proof takes M(M+1)/2 executions. Resending the whole history or rediscovering every old path can grow the same way. Preserve current-candidate coverage with reusable machine checks and shared journeys. Spend fresh judgment on changed decisions and newly interacting behaviors. These growth rates are conditional. [Blueprint §4][blue] · [Current cumulative proof][leadpy] · [Measured guard/build split][time]

The payoff depends on **when a mistake would otherwise be found**. If five features depend on one decision, settling it early can avoid five later changes. Across unrelated features that saving disappears. A new writer can recover why an interface works as it does. A bad assumption copied into every brief also compounds. Lead wins at week scale if better choices, preserved behavior and avoided rework outweigh extra planning, proof and owner time. The three-milestone DoorCount pair shows why that matters. There is no week-scale owner comparison here. [Lead charter C10/C15/C19/C23][charter] · [DoorCount walk][walk] · [Earlier patterns][patterns]

## Proposed experiment — one paired delivery with a real change

Give plain and thin Lead the same **new, rough, two-session dream** from a real owner. Choose work that someone returns to after its status changes. Match builder model, tools, time allowance, access to the owner and one mid-project change. Let each arm use its normal notes and resume. Keep proposals in their own arm. This exercises planning and continuity in two sittings without rehearsing the known DoorCount failure. [Charter goal and continuity][charter] · [DoorCount miss][b]

Before kickoff, the evaluator and owner privately agree on critical journeys, including one interaction between the change and old data, and the premium the owner would pay. Randomize product labels at delivery. Have the owner use both and choose which to keep. Record missed journeys, improvements the owner wants, repair time after the change, total elapsed time, tokens and owner minutes. Lead wins this trial if every critical journey works and the owner chooses a concrete benefit within their agreed premium. A tie at greater cost counts against it. A win justifies a longer trial.
